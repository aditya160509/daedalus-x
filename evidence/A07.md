# A07 — Signed CAD deviation between two designs

**Status:** built (software). This is display-mesh CAD deviation, not measured manufacturing
deviation and not an inspection result.

## Method

For every sampled vertex of revision B, `deviation.ts` finds the closest point on the matched part
of reference A and signs the distance with A's triangle normal:

- positive = the B point lies outside A along A's normal → material added;
- negative = the B point lies inside A → material removed;
- distances are reported in millimetres; zero means coincident with A's surface.

Small parts (≤512 triangles) are searched exhaustively. Dense display meshes use a uniform grid
seeded at the query cell, so the search is bounded; the UI prints the sample count and tolerance so
the approximation is visible. At most 20,000 vertices are sampled across all matched parts.

Parts are matched by name. Repeated names (four wheels) get deterministic `(2)`, `(3)` suffixes in
bounding-box-centre order. Parts present on only one side are listed in the legend, never silently
dropped. A pair is rejected when the part centres differ by more than 5% of the reference diagonal
(minimum 2 mm), and the legend says the revisions must be aligned in the same CAD frame. The colour
scale runs from red (removed) through neutral grey at zero to cyan (added), symmetric at ±range mm,
where the range is the largest sampled absolute deviation (minimum 1 mm).

Three.js is already installed and renders the colour-mapped point layer; no new spatial library was
added.

## UI

Inspect → Compare → Deviation. The A pane stays visible as the reference; the B pane shows the
sampled colour map. The legend states the sign convention, range, sample count, tolerance, method
limitation and unmatched component counts. Turning Compare off also turns deviation off, so stale
colours cannot survive a design change.

## Gates and limits

- No live browser acceptance was captured (dev server was OOM-killed earlier), so the layer is
  type-checked and unit-tested but not screenshot-verified.
- The signed normal convention depends on consistent triangle winding in both GLBs. The alignment
  gate catches translated frames, not a badly wound export; the legend states the convention and
  A remains visible.
- This is not manufacturing deviation: no CMM/scan measurement, datum alignment or inspection
  record is involved.
