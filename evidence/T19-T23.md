# T19 energy audit and T23 test planner
**Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (Jainam's session, branch `next-wave`) · **Date:** 2026-11-02 · **Status:** review

## Built
- `src/daedalus/energy.py` (T19): integrates thrust, drag and rolling force times velocity over a finished M1 or M2 race. Reports thrust work, drag and rolling losses, kinetic energy at the finish, the energy shed while coasting after thrust ends, conversion efficiency, a closure error that checks the integrator, a Sankey-ready `flows()` and a `compare()` between two cars.
- `src/daedalus/testplan.py` (T23): differentiates the TITAN race time with respect to ln(CdA), ln(Crr) and ln(thrust scale), builds the Fisher information of each candidate test and picks tests greedily by D-optimality. Reports each parameter's expected relative uncertainty after every step and lists the parameters the plan leaves poorly identified.
- `tests/test_energy.py` (8 tests) and `tests/test_testplan.py` (8 tests). All car data is synthetic.

## How to run

## Finding that changes the plan
With a synthetic car that finishes 20 m in about 1.2 s, finish time is about 25 times more sensitive to thrust than to drag and about 300 times more than to rolling resistance. Every candidate test (tracks of 5, 10 and 20 m, masses of 50 and 57 g) reads almost the same combination of the three parameters. Result: after four timing runs the thrust uncertainty drops by about 25 times, while drag and rolling resistance stay at about 93% and 100% of their prior uncertainty.
- Timing runs cannot identify CdA or Crr, however the tests are varied. The note in `calibration.py` that calibration "needs at least 3 distinct distances" is true but not enough on its own.
- CdA needs a different measurement: a CFD or wind tunnel value, a coast-down test (no thrust), or a recorded speed trace. Crr needs a coast-down or spin-down test.
- The planner now flags this explicitly (`poorly_identified`). My first version of the docstring and test assumed variety would help; the numbers showed it does not, so both were corrected.

## Decisions and deviations
- The model is a linear-Gaussian approximation around nominal parameters, so the numbers are only as good as those parameters. Rerun the plan as real runs refine them.
- Candidates are track length and mass only. A coast-down test is not a candidate yet because `race_times` does not model a car launched with an initial velocity and no thrust. Adding that is the natural next step.
- The audit needs a finished M1 or M2 result. M0 has no force terms, and a car that does not finish raises `EnergyAuditError`.

## Update: coast-down tests and the role of run-to-run noise (2026-11-02)
- The planner now accepts coast-down tests (`CoastDown`: push the car without a cartridge and time it over a distance). With no thrust, the time depends only on drag and rolling resistance.
- Whether timing runs alone can measure CdA depends on the run-to-run noise. At 5 ms of noise they learn neither CdA nor Crr; at 1 ms they pin CdA to about 5% but still leave Crr near its prior. Real noise from cartridge and launch variation is probably nearer 5 ms; measure it from repeated runs before trusting either case.
- Coast-downs work only with good timing and the right pair of runs: a fast, long coast where drag dominates (about 10 m/s over 8 m) and a slow, short one where rolling dominates (about 2 m/s over 1 m). With 1 ms timing, six such runs give CdA to about 4% and Crr to about 8%. Slow, short coasts alone change the time by only a few milliseconds.
- `/v1/analysis/test-plan` accepts `coast_downs`, and each step now says whether it is a `timing_run` or a `coast_down`.
